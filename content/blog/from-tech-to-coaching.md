# From Data Engineering to Coaching: My Journey

After spending over a decade building data infrastructure and leading engineering teams at major tech companies, I made a decision that surprised many: I transitioned into leadership coaching.

## The Spark

It started during my time as a senior data engineer. I found myself increasingly drawn to the people side of technology - mentoring junior engineers, helping teams navigate organizational changes, and supporting colleagues through career transitions. The technical work was fulfilling, but these moments of human connection felt different. They felt transformative.

## The Transition

Making the leap wasn't easy. I had built a successful career in tech, developed deep expertise in data engineering and machine learning, and earned the respect of my peers. Walking away from that to pursue coaching felt risky.

But I realized I wasn't walking away - I was bringing everything I'd learned with me.

## What I Learned

### 1. Technical Skills Transfer

My background in systems thinking, debugging complex problems, and optimizing processes translates beautifully to coaching. Understanding how systems work - whether they're data pipelines or human organizations - requires the same analytical mindset.

### 2. Empathy is a Superpower

In tech, I learned to empathize with users, stakeholders, and team members. In coaching, empathy is the foundation of everything. It's what allows me to truly understand my clients' challenges and help them find their own solutions.

### 3. Leadership is Universal

The leadership skills I developed in tech - vision-setting, decision-making, team building - are universal. Whether you're leading a data engineering team or coaching executives, the principles remain the same.

## The Education

I pursued formal training at the University of Pennsylvania's Executive Coaching Program. This academic rigor combined with my practical tech experience gave me a unique perspective. I could speak the language of tech leaders because I had been one.

## Why Coaching?

After 10+ years in tech, I discovered that my greatest satisfaction came not from building the most elegant data pipeline or deploying the most sophisticated ML model, but from helping people unlock their potential.

Coaching allows me to do that every day.

## For Those Considering a Similar Path

If you're in tech and feeling drawn to coaching, here's my advice:

1. **Start Small**: Begin by mentoring within your current role. Test the waters.
2. **Get Trained**: Invest in proper coaching education. It's a profession, not just a career pivot.
3. **Leverage Your Background**: Your tech experience is an asset, not something to leave behind.
4. **Trust the Process**: Career transitions are messy. That's normal.

## What's Next

Today, I combine my technical background with coaching expertise to help leaders navigate their own transitions, build effective teams, and create meaningful impact in their organizations.

The journey from data engineering to coaching wasn't a departure from my past - it was the natural evolution of it.
