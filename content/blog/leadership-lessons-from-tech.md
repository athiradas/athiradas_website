# 5 Leadership Lessons I Learned Building Data Teams

Over 10 years of building data engineering teams taught me lessons that extend far beyond technology. Here are five insights that transformed how I think about leadership.

## 1. Clarity Beats Perfection

Early in my career, I spent weeks perfecting technical designs before sharing them with my team. I thought I was being thorough. In reality, I was creating bottlenecks.

**The Lesson**: Your team needs clarity more than they need perfection. A clear direction at 80% complete beats a perfect plan delivered too late.

**In Practice**:
- Share work-in-progress ideas early
- Invite feedback before you're "done"
- Iterate based on team input

## 2. Psychological Safety is Non-Negotiable

The best data pipelines I ever built came from teams that weren't afraid to fail. When engineers felt safe admitting mistakes, we caught bugs earlier, experimented more boldly, and shipped better products.

**The Lesson**: Create an environment where people can take risks without fear of punishment.

**In Practice**:
- Model vulnerability by admitting your own mistakes
- Celebrate good failures (experiments that didn't work but taught us something)
- Never punish someone for raising a concern

## 3. Context Enables Autonomy

I used to assign tasks with detailed instructions. My team delivered exactly what I asked for - nothing more. When I started sharing context instead of instructions, everything changed.

**The Lesson**: When people understand the "why," they find better solutions than you could have prescribed.

**In Practice**:
- Explain the business context, not just the technical requirements
- Share the constraints and trade-offs you're considering
- Ask "What would you do?" instead of "Here's what to do"

## 4. Small Wins Build Momentum

Big data infrastructure projects can take months or years. I learned to break them into two-week milestones that delivered tangible value. This kept the team motivated and stakeholders engaged.

**The Lesson**: Momentum matters more than you think. Small, visible wins fuel team energy and organizational support.

**In Practice**:
- Break large projects into deliverable increments
- Celebrate progress, not just completion
- Make wins visible to the broader organization

## 5. The Best Leaders are Multipliers

I thought my job as a senior engineer was to be the best coder on the team. I was wrong. My job was to make everyone else better.

**The Lesson**: Your impact as a leader is measured by what your team accomplishes, not what you personally deliver.

**In Practice**:
- Invest time in mentoring and coaching
- Remove blockers for your team
- Give away the interesting work, not just the grunt work

## Bringing it Full Circle

These lessons shaped my transition from engineering to coaching. I realized that the most rewarding parts of my tech career weren't about the technology at all - they were about the people.

Now, as a leadership coach, I help other leaders discover and apply these principles in their own contexts. The technology changes, but these human truths remain constant.
